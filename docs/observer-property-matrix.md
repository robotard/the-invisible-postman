# Observer / Property Matrix: Delivery Lifecycle Comparators

**Checkpoint:** 2026-11-16  
**Role:** Working analytical artefact for W0.3/W6 and RQ7–RQ8  
**Status:** Theory only · No implementation · Not audited.

## 1. Purpose and method

This matrix compares what selected prior systems protect, which observers they model, where their formal or empirical coverage stops, and which questions remain for the proposed endpoint-controlled image-carriage architecture. It is not a security ranking. A blank or limited cell means the inspected work does not establish coverage for that stage; it must not be read as proof that a system is vulnerable.

The unit of comparison is the **observer/property claim**, not the system name. In particular, recipient-message unlinkability, sender anonymity, access-pattern privacy, covert-channel undetectability, payload confidentiality, integrity, availability and human behavioural unlinkability are distinct properties.

### Inspection boundary

- **OMR (2022):** full paper inspected; single-recipient public bulletin-board retrieval model.
- **GOMR (2024):** full IACR-hosted paper inspected, including formal models, adversary definitions, constructions, DoS properties, benchmarks and discussion of adjacent private retrieval and MLS work.
- **PerfOMR (2024), SophOMR (2026), InstantOMR (2026):** full papers inspected as recorded in SRC-024, SRC-028 and SRC-029; treated as OMR-family construction/performance variants, not separate end-to-end delivery architectures.
- **Oblivious Signaling (2026):** full USENIX paper inspected; receiver-privacy model, encrypted inbox updates, cost placement and stated limitations.
- **Remise (2026):** full USENIX Security 2026 paper inspected; system model, security definition, DORAM/authorization construction, evaluation and limitations.
- **Dead-drop covert messaging (2024):** full paper inspected; text-based platform pipeline, rendezvous, hints, retrieval, user detection and stated observer exclusions.
- **Image-diffusion covert messaging (2025):** full paper inspected; generated-image latent channel, pre-shared-key assumption, security model and delivery exclusion.

## 3. System-specific boundaries that matter

### OMR and Group OMR

OMR addresses the single-recipient retrieval problem. GOMR extends the model to arbitrary and pre-formed recipient groups, with formal privacy, detection-key unlinkability and denial-of-service notions. The paper distinguishes recipient-message privacy from application payload encryption, contact discovery, authenticated distribution of clue keys, privacy-preserving board posting and the broader messaging protocol. Its adversary can observe public board information and detector-recipient communication and may control/collude with parties other than the protected sender and recipient(s); integrity and DoS claims have their own conditions. Group retrieval is therefore a substantial prior-art result, not a generic feature to be reinvented.

### Oblivious Signaling

Oblivious Signaling changes the cost placement: every sender update is applied uniformly across a recipient anonymity set, reducing the dependence of recipient checking on global traffic at the cost of sender-side work. It protects receiver linkage at the server under its model, but sender anonymity depends on an external anonymous channel. Timing, batch size, registered recipients, read timing and finite inbox retention remain relevant. It is not a general-purpose platform or image-carriage architecture.

### Remise

Remise is a particularly important comparator because it combines anonymous bulletin-board publication and authorized anonymous communication channels over two-server DORAM. Its model includes a global network adversary observing client-server communications, secure channels and cover traffic; privacy requires the two servers not to collude, while integrity requires both servers to follow the protocol. Clients may be malicious and collude with a malicious server. RemiseCC uses bearer authorization tokens distributed out of band; the paper explicitly leaves scalable privacy-preserving token distribution as an open problem. It assumes semi-honest servers and notes that a malicious server may drop writes. It therefore narrows any broad claim to have newly integrated publication, recipient access, relationship privacy and revocation. It does not, however, supply the thesis's image-carrier processing, social-platform transformation, image metadata, capsule decapsulation or human-behaviour analysis.

### Dead-drop and image-diffusion work

The 2024 dead-drop paper already covers a broad text-based public-platform pipeline, including rendezvous, discovery hints, fragmentation, retrieval and user detection, but excludes platform control, fine-grained user actions and IP identifiers in its stated adversary. The 2025 diffusion paper provides formal image-based covert communication but assumes a pre-shared key and leaves delivery to the recipient outside scope. These are related but non-equivalent system models. Wang et al. (2024; SRC-043) add direct multi-image fragment redundancy and reconstruction using robust watermarking; Ker (2007; SRC-044–SRC-045) establishes batch-level pooled steganalysis and a bounded capacity theorem. Neither should be collapsed into the diffusion or dead-drop model.

## 4. Cross-layer observations and research implications

1. **There is no single “delivery privacy” property.** Recipient-message unlinkability, writer-reader relationship privacy, sender anonymity, access-pattern privacy, covert-post detectability and behavioural unlinkability protect different relationships against different observers.
2. **Discovery is not solved by hiding retrieval alone.** OMR/GOMR and Remise rely on authenticated or out-of-band setup material; Oblivious Signaling relies on an external anonymous sender channel. Initial identity/key binding and capability distribution remain separate problems.
3. **Anonymity assumptions are architectural costs.** Two-server systems depend on non-collusion; OMR-family systems depend on their detector/public-board model; cover traffic and fixed epochs have operational costs; dead-drop hints and pre-shared state impose coordination requirements.
4. **Content security and delivery privacy are orthogonal.** Several retrieval systems assume payloads are already encrypted or focus on access control rather than endpoint message semantics. A private retrieval result does not establish sender identity, payload integrity or endpoint safety.
5. **Media carriage introduces an additional observer and transformation layer.** The reviewed OMR, Oblivious Signaling and Remise papers do not provide a social-image-platform transformation or forensic model. The dead-drop and diffusion papers address different carrier constructions and assumptions.
6. **Human behaviour remains weakly covered.** None of the reviewed cryptographic retrieval systems establishes that varied carriers or hidden access patterns prevent correlation through human routines or social-engineering probes.
7. **The strongest potential analytical value is in assumption compatibility, not component accumulation.** The research must show a concrete cross-layer incompatibility, leakage path or evaluation method that is not already exposed by these systems' own models. If it cannot, the emergent proposition must be narrowed, displaced or abandoned.



### 4.1 Adversarial discovery-event pressure test

The observer comparison must not assume that publication, indexing and retrieval services follow the protocol or expose only their intended interface. The following counterexamples extend the matrix without claiming that any cited system is vulnerable in the same way or that a mitigation has been selected:

| Adversarial case | Relevant observer combination | Property placed under pressure | Evidence boundary / test question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap substitution or race | Bootstrap-channel operator; intended peer; network observer | Initial peer/key/locator authenticity and privacy of setup | What independently authenticated binding detects substitution or a changed key? Which identities and timings does the setup channel expose? |
| Malicious or selective publisher | Publisher + platform; potentially index | Publication provenance, object availability and event correlation | Can false, stale, duplicated or selectively transformed objects be injected? What endpoint checks reject them, and what cannot be inferred about omitted objects? |
| Index equivocation, poisoning or omission | Index/board + different recipients; index + network observer | Candidate-view consistency, completeness and retrieval privacy | Can recipients be shown different candidate sets? Is there a specified, independently checkable consistency mechanism, or only a service response? |
| Retrieval manipulation | Retrieval service + capability holder; service + network observer | Access authorisation, pertinence privacy and availability | Can the service replay, substitute, delay or withhold responses while recording capability use, source and timing? What does an authenticated response actually attest? |
| Cross-role collusion | Publisher+index; index+retrieval; publisher+retrieval; all roles; add network/platform where credible | Correlation across otherwise separate events | Which object IDs, capabilities, account/device identifiers, source addresses, sizes, timestamps and retries join records? Are operators genuinely independent? |
| Retention and later disclosure | Any service role + platform/account records; later recipient of retained logs | Longitudinal unlinkability and deletion claims | Which logs, backups, moderation and telemetry survive object/locator expiry? Is retention observed, documented or unknown? |
| Suppression and ambiguous silence | Publisher, index, retrieval and network roles; recipient | Availability and ability to attribute a missing event | Can non-publication, index omission, suppression, ordinary delay and route failure be distinguished within the declared observation window? |

The comparison must separate four evidence classes: endpoint-authenticated protocol facts, service-reported events, externally observed metadata and analyst-inferred correlations. A successful index query does not establish index completeness; a missing result does not establish non-publication; a capability-use record does not establish the human identity of its user; and endpoint rejection does not identify which intermediary altered or withheld an object. Provider policy is not observed retention behaviour. Any claim about absence, deletion or suppression must state its vantage, time window and evidential basis.

This test is a refinement of the common observer model, not a new universal attack claim. If a profile excludes collusion or assumes trusted service behaviour, record that as an explicit assumption and examine the cost of relying on it. The application of these seven cases to the six named image, file-host, protocol and mixnet profiles is recorded in the [route-comparison framework §5.8](route-comparison-framework.md#58-cross-profile-adversarial-discovery-comparison) and synthesised in thesis §8.8; preserve the profile-specific evidence classes rather than treating this observer matrix as proof of deployed behaviour.

## 6. Current emergent proposition (provisional)

> A useful analysis of endpoint-controlled messaging over replaceable external carriage may need to test the compatibility of endpoint message semantics, private recipient discovery/access, media-carrier transformation and externally observable human/network behaviour under one explicit observer model; individually valid protections may leave cross-layer leakage or incompatible assumptions when composed.

This proposition is deliberately weaker and more conditional than a claim that the project has already achieved a novel whole-lifecycle privacy analysis. The additional prior art makes a generic lifecycle claim untenable. The proposition must still be challenged by further integrated anonymous-messaging, mix-network, private-signaling and image-carriage work.

## 9. Image-platform steganography, multi-carrier linkage, active sanitization and animated-carrier prior art

The image-carriage column must be read against a mature and active technical literature, not treated as an empty space. SRC-033 (MRAS, 2026) and SRC-034 (DRAS, 2026) address JPEG robustness under social-network processing, with reported real-platform tests. SRC-035 compares robust methods under simulated and practical social-network channels. SRC-036 studies image processing designed to destroy robust hidden payloads while preserving image quality. SRC-037 surveys image embedding and detection research.

| Property | Established research coverage | Consequence for this project |
|---|---|---|
| JPEG recompression survival | MRAS and DRAS propose methods targeted at social-platform recompression; earlier comparative work measures real service behaviour. | Payload survival alone is not a novel contribution. Evaluate the specific route and transformations independently. |
| Multiple/unknown channel operations | Comparative work identifies gaps between simplified compression models and real platform channels. | Name and version every platform path; do not extrapolate a single JPEG quality factor to an entire platform. |
| Active payload removal | Learned image-processing attacks have been proposed to remove hidden data while maintaining image quality. | Treat carrier transformation as potentially adversarial; extraction success must be tested after realistic and adversarial processing. |
| Steganalysis | A broad survey covers embedding and detection approaches. | Declare detector, dataset, payload, cover source, operating point and false-positive/negative measures. |
| End-to-end message semantics | These carrier-focused works do not by themselves establish sender authentication, recipient authentication, freshness, context binding or secure reassembly. | Keep endpoint security claims separate from carrier robustness and concealment. |
| Discovery and observer privacy | The cited carrier works do not establish private recipient discovery, anonymous retrieval or protection from platform account/engagement records. | Evaluate those as distinct lifecycle properties, without assuming the absence of coverage in one paper establishes novelty. |

The route-comparison study must separate extraction reliability, detectability, endpoint authenticity/integrity and observer privacy. These are not interchangeable outcomes.

The completed full-text inspection of SRC-035 and SRC-037 sharpens this matrix:

- **Transformation is not one variable.** JPEG recompression, resizing, transcoding, image editing, metadata rewriting and active payload removal are different transformations and may compose in an unknown order.
- **Laboratory channel models are not platform profiles.** Qiao et al. report divergent outcomes between simulated compression and their tested Facebook, Twitter and WeChat paths, including materially different WeChat original-image and default-send behaviour. The sample and client configuration are bounded to that study.
- **Robustness does not imply concealment.** Extraction error and complete recovery measure payload survival; PSNR/SSIM address image quality; detector error addresses a particular steganalysis setup. None establishes endpoint authenticity or privacy from platform/account observers.
- **Steganalysis generalisation is limited.** The SRC-037 survey's taxonomy and cross-paper synthesis caution against assuming one detector generalises across embedding schemes, cover sources and processing pipelines.
- **Active removal is a separate adversarial capability.** SRC-036 describes an image-quality-preserving learned attack against robust hidden payloads. Its full-text details remain only partially inspected, so this matrix records the capability class, not a universal attacker guarantee.

The image-route evidence ledger must therefore name the exact client path and distinguish source-reported facts, direct measurements (if later authorised), and unresolved assumptions. It must also record safe receiver behaviour when extraction is incomplete or authentication fails.


## 8. Integrated comparator · Echomix / Katzenpost

Echomix is included as an integrated messaging system, not merely a mix-network transport. The full paper (SRC-032) combines a global and active adversary model, decoy/application traffic coupling, anonymous service access, BACAP capability-derived storage locations, Pigeonhole asynchronous messaging, courier/replica separation, reliability and backfill semantics, tombstones, and role-collusion analysis.

| Lifecycle/property boundary | Echomix / Katzenpost coverage | Remaining comparison question for image carriage |
|---|---|---|
| Endpoint message protection | BACAP provides derived per-message encryption and signatures; read/write capabilities distinguish access roles under its model. | Compare exact identity verification, session establishment, sender authentication and message-context semantics without assuming those responsibilities are absent. |
| Publication and service access | Clients send through gateways and layered mixes to services; service access is traffic-coupled with decoys. | What does an ordinary image platform expose through account identity, public post, file metadata, moderation and hosting records? |
| Discovery and retrieval | Pseudorandom capability-derived box IDs, blinded addressing, courier/replica separation and authenticated read capability. | Does the image route require a different discovery mechanism, and what new locator, account, timing or access-pattern leakage does it create? |
| Network observation | Global passive observer and active disruption/compromise are explicit; traffic patterns and collusion are analysed. | Separate network-level guarantees from platform-side media and account observations. |
| Carrier transformation | Not an ordinary user-selected social-image pipeline. | Test resizing, recompression, transcoding, metadata rewriting, format conversion, moderation and extraction failure on named platforms. |
| Reliability and lifecycle | Courier acknowledgements, controlled retries, backfills, tombstones, finite storage retention and user-triggered signalling are considered. | Compare message-set completeness, retry visibility, platform deletion, carrier expiry and route-failure semantics under common conditions. |
| Human behaviour | Correlated online routines and statistical disclosure are explicitly acknowledged. | Determine whether image publication/retrieval adds distinct human interaction observables; avoid claiming human correlation is unstudied generally. |
| Costs and residual leakage | Latency, bandwidth, continuous traffic, storage, server roles, query patterns and specific collusion combinations are part of the design analysis. | Compare image-route capacity, platform dependency, user effort, discoverability, transformation fragility and observable delivery state. |

### What this changes

1. A broad integrated delivery-privacy account is not a defensible differentiator: Echomix already integrates network anonymity, messaging, discovery/storage, reliability and metadata analysis.
2. The project's train boundary must be compared with Echomix's gateway, mix, service, courier and replica responsibilities. Responsibility separation alone is not a distinct contribution.
3. The remaining potentially distinct boundary is ordinary image-platform carriage and its interaction with a common endpoint-protected message object: media transformations, file-level metadata, account/engagement traces, moderation, hosting logs and extraction failure.
4. That boundary is a research target, not a finding of originality. It must be compared with image steganography, platform forensics, dead-drop pipelines and external-service integration.
5. A useful evaluation may compare the same endpoint-protected semantics across image, file/attachment, conventional messaging and a mixnet-backed messaging baseline, while retaining each route's native guarantees and assumptions.

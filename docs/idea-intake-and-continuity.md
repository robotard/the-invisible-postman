# Idea Intake and Contributor Continuity

**Purpose:** Prevent useful ideas, contributions and review knowledge from being lost when participation is absent, intermittent, pseudonymous or dormant.  
**Status:** Proposed project process · Manual · Not externally reviewed  
**Project assurance:** Theory only · No implementation · Not audited.

## 1. The three continuity risks

The project explicitly plans for three conditions:

1. **No external contributors:** The work may remain single-maintainer for a period. This must not be disguised as peer review, and it must not prevent the project from preserving ideas, recording uncertainty or preparing reviewable work.
2. **Absent or stale contributors:** A contributor may become unavailable, stop responding, lose access, or return after a long interval. Their earlier work and attribution do not expire merely because they are inactive. Their silence is not approval, rejection, withdrawal or consent.
3. **Good ideas being missed:** Useful suggestions may arrive outside the current work unit, conflict with the current direction, be submitted anonymously, or be only partly formed. Every substantive idea needs a durable capture route and a later disposition.

These are normal operating conditions, not exceptional edge cases.

## 2. Intake routes

Accept substantive ideas through GitHub issues or discussions, pull requests, review comments tied to an exact artifact, research notes, or a maintainer-created record of a verbal/off-platform suggestion. Such transcriptions must be labelled as awaiting contributor confirmation.

An idea must not be discarded solely because it arrives through the wrong channel. Transfer it into the durable register while preserving origin and date. Do not publish private correspondence or personal information without permission.

## 3. Minimum idea record

Each substantive idea receives a stable ID and, where known:
- date received and source channel;
- contributor's chosen name or pseudonym, or anonymous status;
- original wording or a faithful summary;
- problem or opportunity identified;
- affected research question, threat, architecture component or claim;
- proposed benefit and possible downside;
- evidence or references supplied;
- related ideas, prior decisions and possible duplicates;
- status and next review point;
- attribution preference and confirmation state.

Do not require a fully designed solution before accepting a criticism or useful question. A reviewer may identify a real problem without being obliged to design the replacement.

## 4. Triage states

Use explicit states:
- **Captured:** Preserved, not yet assessed.
- **Needs clarification:** A question is necessary to understand the idea.
- **Queued:** Relevant, awaiting a scheduled review.
- **Under examination:** Being compared with evidence, alternatives and affected claims.
- **Accepted:** Incorporated in whole or in part, with exact scope recorded.
- **Adapted:** The underlying insight is used in a materially different form; explain the change.
- **Rejected:** Not adopted, with reasons and evidence where available.
- **Deferred:** Potentially valuable, but dependent on scope, evidence or resources.
- **Duplicate/linked:** Connected to an existing record; retain the new provenance and link it rather than silently deleting it.
- **Withdrawn by contributor:** The contributor has requested withdrawal of their proposal or future use where practicable. Record what can and cannot be removed from public history.
- **Dormant:** No current activity; the record remains available and may be reactivated.

A captured idea is not an accepted idea. An accepted idea is not automatically a validated claim.

## 5. Review and resurfacing

1. Capture ideas as they arrive; do not rely on memory, chat history or an inbox.
2. Link each idea to the relevant thesis claim, W1–W4 section, threat, decision or open question.
3. Triage at each meaningful research checkpoint and before a dependent decision is closed.
4. Revisit queued and deferred ideas at the next relevant work unit, not only at arbitrary calendar intervals.
5. Before publication or major thesis integration, scan the complete register for captured, queued, deferred and dormant items.
6. Record why an idea was not taken forward. Do not silently delete inconvenient alternatives.
7. Where a later idea duplicates an earlier one, preserve both provenance records and explain the relationship.

The maintainer should periodically challenge the register itself: are there missing perspectives, unrepresented disciplines, alternative use cases or assumptions that have never been tested?

## 6. Contributor absence and return

- A contributor's prior accepted work remains attributed according to the agreed record; inactivity does not erase contribution.
- Do not treat an unanswered request as approval, consent to attribution, endorsement or ratification.
- If attribution confirmation is unavailable, mark it pending or unconfirmed; do not guess a preferred name, affiliation, CRediT role or endorsement.
- A contributor may return and correct attribution, add a response, challenge a prior disposition or propose a superseding idea. Preserve original history and record the update.
- A material change to a proposal after review requires renewed review of the changed scope.
- A dormant contributor does not retain an automatic veto over unrelated future work. Their material unresolved objection remains visible and must be considered in the relevant decision.
- If the contributor cannot be reached, the maintainer may make a project decision only under the published governance rules, with the absence and its effect recorded. Silence never counts as a vote.

## 7. No-contributor operating mode

When no external contributors are active:
- continue author-led critical examination and source reconciliation;
- label all such work as maintainer analysis, not peer review;
- keep external-review gates visibly pending;
- do not self-appoint independent reviewers or create synthetic endorsements;
- avoid adopting practices that explicitly require independent ratification;
- keep proposals and decisions reversible and versioned;
- prepare clear, bounded review packets so future contributors can examine exact claims and versions efficiently.

A project can make honest progress without external contributors. It cannot claim external validation in their absence.

## 8. Attribution and intellectual provenance

Distinguish idea origin, substantive analysis or evidence, drafting or editing, review, approval of an exact version, ratification of a governance decision, and maintenance/integration.

Use CRediT where suitable, with plain-language descriptions and contributor confirmation. Credit the actual accepted portion. If an idea is adapted, explain the transformation. If a contributor only raised a question, do not inflate that into authorship of the eventual solution; equally, do not erase the intellectual prompt that led to it.

No contribution is attributed to a person without a defensible provenance record. No person is described as endorsing the whole project because one contribution was accepted.

## 9. Relationship to the adopted baseline

An idea can influence research without becoming a project-wide practice. A reusable practice must pass the separate proposal, evidence, independent review and ratification process in the Collaborative Research Governance Framework. The adopted-practices index changes only through a recorded decision and reviewed merge.

## 10. Assurance

This is a proposed manual process. It has not yet been exercised with an external contributor, and no automated intake, reminder, attribution or triage system is implemented.

**Theory only · No implementation · Not audited.**
